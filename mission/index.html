The mission

I started this shop as a protest.

I don’t believe any living creature should suffer for what someone wears. So I don’t work with real fur. The fur here is faux, every time, on purpose.

The effect was never the animal

Look at the scarf beside this and ask what real fur would have added. The depth is there. The pile is there. It moves the way it is supposed to move when you turn your head. All of it came off a cone of polyamide.

That is the argument I am making with every piece. The things people buy fur for — the loft, the weight, the way light sits in it — are effects, and effects can be built. Nothing has to be farmed to get them.

Faux fur is a synthetic. It is plastic, and I am not going to dress that up as something it isn’t. But it is not an animal, and that is the trade I have chosen. The scarf above is 60% faux fur; the cat-ear beanie is 50%. Both are written up at full percentage on the materials page, next to everything else I know about them.

№Where I stop

The honest limits of a young label
№ 01

No blanket ethics label

I avoid real fur. I can’t audit a wool or alpaca supply chain end to end, so I won’t put a sweeping claim on this shop that I couldn’t prove if you asked me to.

№ 02

Not certified ethical

No animal-welfare standard covers the yarns I use. One yarn holds a human-safety certificate, and I say exactly what that does and does not mean on the materials page.

№ 03

Not a donation scheme

I don’t take a cut for a cause, because I haven’t set one up. If that ever changes, it will come with the amount and the receipts.

What this shop is for

I am not trying to win an argument with anybody. I am trying to make things good enough that nobody feels they gave something up by choosing them. If someone wears a piece of mine all winter and never once wishes it were fur, that is the whole job done.

To me, every piece sold is a small step toward a world I want to live in. I can't promise it changes much. But it's the step I get to make, and making these is what I want to do with my life.

The collection lives on Etsy.

Every piece that is still available is there. When one sells, it’s gone — the next will be something else.
