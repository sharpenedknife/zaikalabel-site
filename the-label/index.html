The label · one maker, Poland

Zaika is Zlata.

I'm Zlata. I hand-crochet every piece myself, in Poland. There is no team and no production line — if a piece exists, it's because I sat with it for hours.

I choose yarns for one thing: how the finished surface behaves. A halo that moves when you do. A pile deep enough to put your face in. A stitch that holds its own shape instead of lying flat. Texture is the reason these pieces exist — not warmth, and not a pattern I bought.

Nothing repeats. I don't work from a pattern I could run again, and I don't restock. A scarf takes me 11–14 hours; a top can take 16. When a piece sells, its listing closes for good. If something is still on Etsy, it's available. If it's gone, I can make you something in the same family — but it will not be the same object.

Zaika is a young label, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. What I can promise is that I answer every message myself, and that what arrives is exactly the piece in the photographs — because there is only one.

I make these because making them is what I want to do with my life. That part isn't a strategy.
